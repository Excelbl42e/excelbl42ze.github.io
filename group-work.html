Working in a group

SCIS111 Communication and Study Skills group presentation, October 2026 · Using Tuckman's stages of group development

Introduction

This semester I was in Group 1.1. The groups were allocated through the module by 18 September 2026, and we were added to our group on Microsoft Teams. There were six of us: me, Michael Chitonho, Medo Bathaedi, Sibusiso Tshuma, Theetso (Bafo) Baforegi and Maatla Balo. Our task was to run a simulated Student Board meeting and give a 10-minute presentation, plus about three minutes of Q&A, proposing a First-Year Student Success Plan for BAC. I was the chairperson, so I led the project and ran the meeting. Below I go through how our group developed using Tuckman's model.

Forming

I already knew everyone in the group because we're in the same class, so we didn't have to break the ice like strangers would. The first job was deciding who would do what. As chairperson, I made sure the work was split equally: everyone got two objectives and their own slides. Michael was our secretary and kept the team process record and deadlines. I picked Medo as the proposer because her objective, the solutions, matched that role. Maatla researched the challenges first-year students face, and Sibusiso, Bafo and Michael covered the overview, purpose and team process. I did Slide 9, a practical communication demonstration, and shared Slide 12, the group dynamics reflection, with Sibusiso. We used a WhatsApp group to organise ourselves.

Storming

We didn't have big arguments. I think that's because the roles were clear from the start. Everyone owned specific slides, so nobody could sit back and let the others do the work (social loafing). Our real problem was scheduling. We planned a progress check for Thursday 1 October, but some members couldn't make it. We didn't go ahead without them. We compromised and moved it to Saturday 3 October so everyone could be there. Another thing I remember is that our presentation script had a line making it sound like Bafo spoke less than everyone else. I thought that was unfair to him, so I had it taken out. Everyone's contribution deserved the same credit.

Norming

Once the roles were sorted, we agreed on the slides, the deadlines and how the presentation would run. People started helping each other outside their own parts. Bafo helped Michael with the Team Process Record, and Sibusiso helped me with the reflection slide. Having a chairperson and a secretary kept us organised. Everyone got a say, and we had a record of what we decided.

Performing

We got to the performing stage when we built and practised the whole presentation together, including working side by side in the computer lab. Our slides went problem, then solution, so Maatla's and Medo's parts had to link up properly. In my demonstration I compared two ways a lecturer could announce a deadline change. One was a message in a busy WhatsApp group, where it gets buried in the chat. The other was a clear Outlook email that students have to acknowledge. As chairperson I also ran the agenda, handed over between speakers and led the Q&A.

Adjourning

After we presented, we submitted our work with an acknowledgement confirming that every member contributed. The group finished on good terms. I think that's because the work was shared fairly and we sorted out the scheduling problem by compromising instead of letting it build up.

What I learned

First, clear roles from the start stop most conflict and unequal participation before they happen. When everyone knows what they're responsible for, there's less to argue about. Second, a group works better when it adjusts to its members. Moving one meeting cost us two days, but it kept everyone involved. Third, I noticed something about our own process. Our key message was that important information should go through a proper, recorded channel, but we organised ourselves mostly on WhatsApp, the same informal channel we were criticising. It worked for six people, but it showed me how easily important messages get lost. In future group work, and at work, I'll agree roles and meeting times at the first meeting, keep a written record of decisions, and put deadlines in a channel where they can't get buried.
