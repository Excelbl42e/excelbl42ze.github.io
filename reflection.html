Reflection: My Academic Journey So Far

Posted November 2026 · Semester 1, BSc Computer Systems Engineering

Starting out

The biggest difference I noticed when I moved from secondary school to BAC was freedom. In secondary school my study time was mostly decided for me, but at BAC I am free to study the way I want, and my work schedule is more flexible around my classes as well. What I did not expect was how hard it would be to bring what I know from work into university. At work, recognising a product or knowing how something works is usually enough. At university, rigour is expected instead: I have to show my reasoning step by step and prove what I know, not just acknowledge it. Learning to work to that standard has been one of my main adjustments this year.

Managing my time

To be honest, I do not fit everything together well yet. Every time I pick up one task, I tend to neglect the others, because multitasking is difficult for me with ADHD. What I have found is that when I do take on a task, I break it down and list its objectives before I start. Working through that list makes sure the task is done properly and to a good quality, and that quality cancels out most of the negatives of the things I put aside.

Study habits

This year I changed how I study by combining three methods. The first is the Pareto principle, the idea that roughly 80 percent of results come from 20 percent of the effort, so I identify the topics that matter most and put my energy there first. The second is the Pomodoro technique, where I study in short focused intervals with breaks in between instead of long sessions that drain me. The third is genetic learning: for maths and engineering modules, I learn a topic by finding out how it came to be, the problem people were trying to solve and how the idea developed. Understanding where an idea comes from helps it make sense to me, so I can rebuild it rather than memorise it.

Growth

One setback this year was that as I took on more responsibilities, between university, work and my own projects, it became hard to manage every part of my life properly because of executive dysfunction. Things I planned to finish quickly took much longer, and other areas slipped while I caught up. What I learnt from this is that I cannot accomplish big targets in short periods of time. I now have to account for slack time in my plans, leaving space for things to take longer than expected instead of planning as if everything will go perfectly.

Moving forward

Next semester I plan to go for a proper assessment and diagnosis for my ADHD, because it affects my learning, my studying and my lifestyle in a big way. I can honestly say that I am capable, but there is a bottleneck holding me back, and getting the right support is the step that will help me work at the level I know I can reach. Alongside that, I will keep breaking tasks into objectives, keep using the Pareto principle, Pomodoro and genetic learning, and build slack time into every plan I make.
