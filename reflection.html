My academic journey so far

November 2026 · Semester 1, BSc Computer Systems Engineering

Starting out

The biggest change from secondary school to BAC is freedom. In secondary school my study time was mostly decided for me. At BAC I can study the way I want, and my work hours are more flexible around my classes too. The hard part has been taking what I know from work and using it at university. At work, if I recognise a product and know how it works, that's usually enough. Here they want rigour. I have to show my reasoning step by step and prove what I know, not just say I know it. Getting used to that has been one of my biggest adjustments this year.

Managing my time

Honestly, I don't fit everything together well yet. Every time I pick up one task, I end up neglecting the others, because multitasking is really hard for me with ADHD. What helps is that when I do pick up a task, I break it down and write a list of objectives before I start. Going through that list makes sure the task gets done properly, and doing it well makes up for most of what I fell behind on while I was focused on it.

Study habits

This year I changed how I study, and I use three methods together. The first is the Pareto principle, the 80/20 rule. Most of the results come from a small part of the work, so I find the topics that matter most and do those first. The second is the Pomodoro technique. I study in short, focused blocks with breaks in between, instead of long sessions that wear me out. The third is genetic learning. For maths and engineering modules, I learn a topic by finding out how it came to be: what problem people were trying to solve and how the idea developed. When I know where an idea comes from, it makes sense to me, and I can work it out again instead of just memorising it.

Growth

A setback this year was that the more responsibilities I took on, between university, work and my own projects, the harder it got to manage everything properly, because of executive dysfunction. Things I thought would be quick took much longer, and other parts of my life slipped while I caught up. What I learnt is that I can't hit big targets in short periods of time. Now I plan with slack time, so there's room for things to take longer than I expect instead of planning as if everything will go perfectly.

Moving forward

Next semester I plan to get properly assessed and diagnosed for my ADHD, because it affects how I learn, how I study and how I live in a big way. I know I'm capable, but there's a bottleneck holding me back, and getting the right support is how I start working at the level I know I can reach. I'll also keep breaking my tasks into objectives, keep using the 80/20 rule, Pomodoro and genetic learning, and plan with slack time.
